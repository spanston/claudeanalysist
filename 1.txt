Source guide
This technical guide explores the intricate world of Elliott Wave theory, focusing specifically on the corrective structures that interrupt and refine primary market trends. The text details the anatomical differences between simple corrections, such as zig-zags and flats, and complex corrections like triangles and "double threes," which are categorized as either corrections through price or corrections through time. A significant portion of the material is dedicated to the behavior of Wave B, illustrating how its predictability and strength shift depending on whether it moves with or against the dominant trend direction. By using case studies and alteration guidance, the source ultimately teaches traders how to interpret these volatile patterns to better anticipate when a trend is likely to resume its original momentum.

Waves B: Your Key Takeaways from this lesson:
Waves B may be shaped as any corrective structure
Inside a corrective A-B-C structure wave B moves in direction of the main trend. That makes wave B stronger and it can reach a new extreme over the one established by the preceding impulsive wave
Inside impulsive waves 1, 3 and 5 subwave b is more predictable because it moves in counter direction to the main trend. As a result it is weak and can’t violate the starting point of preceding impulsive subwave a
Inside a corrective A-B-C micro structure of wave B quite often gets really ugly making it very difficult to micro count a-b-c move. Those ugly waves b often get shaped as quick emotional spikes with hardly recognizable micro structure. More often than not waves b represent sudden change in market sentiment in reaction to unexpected important or breaking news or events

Corrective Waves Combinations Prerequisite conditions
At the top of an impulsive wave bulls feel euphoria and bears throw in towel meaning that the last short sellers close their shorts at huge loss to avoid further losses in case rally continues which look at this time as a sure thing. After a strong rally in an impulsive wave time comes for a correction. The ultimate goal of any correction is to alter sentiment of market participants.
After a strong rally bulls sometimes feel so confident and bears are so hopeless that they can not drive price in a strong corrective counter trend wave. As a result we get Regular Flat, Running Flat or Triangle corrective pattern.
Because bears in that case fail to drive price for a substantial distance bulls keep holding tight to their longs and new short sellers are not attracted to the market. That weak countertrend move does not change sentiment in the way needed for continuation of the rally!
To relaunch the rally there should be enough short sellers whose shorts will be powering rally once a new high is reached!
To relaunch the rally prices should get lower to attract new buyers who would drive new rally. Or bulls waiting for a correction to buy on weakness should get confident that the lows are in and they should buy and stop waiting for lower prices.

Waves B: Your Key Takeaways from this lesson:
Waves B may be shaped as any corrective structure
Inside a corrective A-B-C structure wave B moves in direction of the main trend. That makes wave B stronger and it can reach a new extreme over the one established by the preceding impulsive wave
Inside impulsive waves 1, 3 and 5 subwave b is more predictable because it moves in counter direction to the main trend. As a result it is weak and can’t violate the starting point of preceding impulsive subwave a
Inside a corrective A-B-C micro structure of wave B quite often gets really ugly making it very difficult to micro count a-b-c move. Those ugly waves b often get shaped as quick emotional spikes with hardly recognizable micro structure. More often than not waves b represent sudden change in market sentiment in reaction to unexpected important or breaking news or events

Corrective Waves Combinations Prerequisite conditions
At the top of an impulsive wave bulls feel euphoria and bears throw in towel meaning that the last short sellers close their shorts at huge loss to avoid further losses in case rally continues which look at this time as a sure thing. After a strong rally in an impulsive wave time comes for a correction. The ultimate goal of any correction is to alter sentiment of market participants.
After a strong rally bulls sometimes feel so confident and bears are so hopeless that they can not drive price in a strong corrective counter trend wave. As a result we get Regular Flat, Running Flat or Triangle corrective pattern.
Because bears in that case fail to drive price for a substantial distance bulls keep holding tight to their longs and new short sellers are not attracted to the market. That weak countertrend move does not change sentiment in the way needed for continuation of the rally!
To relaunch the rally there should be enough short sellers whose shorts will be powering rally once a new high is reached!
To relaunch the rally prices should get lower to attract new buyers who would drive new rally. Or bulls waiting for a correction to buy on weakness should get confident that the lows are in and they should buy and stop waiting for lower prices.

1 Five Wave down fractal has six yellow boxes while the Actual Chart of ES mini has
only four boxes.
2 On the Five Wave down fractal two boxes in the middle are the largest ones.
3 The reason why the Double Three structure is very difficult to trade is that we can prematurely call
the bottom after the first (a)-(b)-(c) move down. There are no signs that could tell us in advance whether the first (a)-(b)-(c) move down will be all of the correction or it can extend in the second (a)-(b)-(c) structure. The extension into the second (a)-(b)-(c) structure happens when the first decline was not strong enough to change sentiment in the market. If majority of bulls still believe that this drop is a temporary pullback it is very difficult for the market to take off. The market remains too heavy. The sentiment of the market participants changes from the greed and euphoria at the top to fear and panic at the bottom. The market keeps moving down until the crowd is scared enough to dump their longs and shorting the market starts to be considered a “no-brainer” type of trade.
