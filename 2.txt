Source guide
This technical guide details the application of Harmonic Elliott Wave theory, focusing on the mathematical relationships and structural markers used to identify market fractals. The text emphasizes retracement and projection ratios, such as the critical 176.4% minimum extension for Wave 3, to calculate price targets and validate the completion of specific trends. By utilizing case studies from stocks like Microsoft and Gilead, it demonstrates how corrective and impulsive waves interact through a series of Fibonacci-based percentages. Rather than using the traditional, exhaustive bottom-up counting method, the author advocates for a more efficient Top-to-Down process, which involves zooming out to recognize distinctive visual markers before verifying them against core rules. This systematic approach serves as a practical framework for traders to predict future price levels and maintain structural integrity when labeling complex market cycles.

The Basic Building Blocks of Any Price Chart: Down A-B-C Fractal
A-B-C fractals: Your Key Takeaways from this lesson:
Price never travels in a straight line for too long, it travels in zig-zags shaped as A-B-C
Wave A is Action, Wave B is reaction to the first move, Wave C is resolution to both preceding waves A and B
In up looking A-B-C fractal the starting point of the wave A is the main support for the whole A-B-C up fractal. Reactionery Wave B down may come back up to the lowest point of A but never should violate it
In down looking A-B-C fractal the starting point of the wave A is the main resistance for the whole A-B-C down fractal. Reactionery Wave B up may come back up to the highest point of A but never should violate it

How our brain recognizes familiar objectsKEY TAKEAWAYS
1 Our brain does not need to see a whole picture and all the details of an object to
recognize it. The object may be partially covered but brain will make an educated guess about what is hidden or covered.
2 To make a guess our brain has to be trained on numerous samples. To recognize an
object by the look of its part you first need to see hundreds of samples of such objects. You brain automatically stores those samples in your long term memory and categorizes them. In particular in distinguishes key features of objects, something that helps us to distinguish a cat from a dog, a sedun from a truck, a man from a woman.
Fractal forecasting is based on the very same principle. First we teach our brain about specific details of the main fractals or patterns we track. Second we train our brain on numerous examples where actual charts are labelled. We add numbers to recognize specific waves or segments of fractals. Finally we can feed to our brain the look of incomplete fractals. That should help it to recognize unfolding fractals before their completion.
